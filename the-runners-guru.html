Now in testing

The Biometric Fueling Project

Your own body data, turned into a clearer fueling plan.

Instead of relying only on general rules like “60–90 g of carbs per hour”, it combines continuous glucose readings, sweat sodium and sweat rate from a wearable sweat sensor, and heart rate and route data from a GPS watch. It then shows, hour by hour, how much carbohydrate, sodium and fluid matched what your body actually used during a training run.

No sensors? Start with a quick plan based on your body weight, time on feet and conditions, then replace those estimates with measured data as you train.

Continuous glucose

Continuous glucose readings show how your body responds to what you eat while you run.

Sweat sodium and rate

A wearable sweat sensor measures how much sodium and fluid you actually lose.

Heart rate and route

Heart rate and route data from your GPS watch put the effort and the terrain in context.

Safety comes first

  • Fluid suggestions never exceed your measured sweat losses.
  • Gaps in the data are shown as gaps, never guessed.
  • Every estimate is clearly labelled as an estimate.

Where it stands: the project is being built and tested on real races, working toward Cocodona 250 in May 2027. It will be shared with other runners only after review by a sports dietitian and legal advisors.
